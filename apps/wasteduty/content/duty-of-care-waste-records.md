# Duty of care waste records: what to keep, for how long, and how to show them

_Rules version: EPA 1990 s.34; Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2011; The Digital Waste Tracking (England) Regulations 2026 (draft SI) · Last reviewed: 8 August 2026_

The waste duty of care is the oldest obligation on your site and the one inspections keep coming back to. **Section 34 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990** ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/43/section/34)) requires anyone who produces, carries, keeps, treats or disposes of controlled waste to take all reasonable measures to:

- prevent unauthorised or harmful deposit, treatment or disposal;
- prevent the escape of waste from their control;
- transfer waste only to an authorised person; and
- ensure the waste is adequately described when transferred.

Breach is a criminal offence (EPA 1990 s.34(6)) `[VERIFY: current maximum penalty on summary conviction / indictment before quoting figures]`. The duty is discharged by what you _do_ — but demonstrated by what you _keep_.

## The records that evidence the duty

1. **Transfer documentation** — written description per transfer under the Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2011 ([SI 2011/988](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2011/988)); retained two years for non-hazardous waste `[VERIFY: reg. 35 retention citation]`, three years for hazardous consignment notes `[VERIFY: Hazardous Waste Regulations 2005 citation]`.
2. **Carrier authorisation checks** — dated evidence that each carrier's CBDU registration was verified against the EA public register.
3. **Waste descriptions and EWC classifications** — including the reasoning for non-obvious classifications.
4. **Site records** — permit reference, permitted EWC codes, tonnage limits, returns submitted.
5. **From 1 October 2026: DWT receipt records** — every load received at a permitted facility, recorded within two working days ([The Digital Waste Tracking (England) Regulations 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348282726)), with the submission reference kept against your own record.

## What "showing them" looks like

An EA officer's questions are predictable: _show me the movements for this period; show me who carried them; show me the carrier checks; show me where load X went._ The difference between a good inspection and a bad one is rarely whether the work was done — it's whether the records can be produced quickly, coherently and without gaps. That's the standard to build for: any movement traceable to its carrier check, its classification, its evidence (weighbridge ticket, photos) and its DWT submission reference in minutes.

## Common failure modes at 1–25-person operators

- Records live in one person's head, inbox or van.
- Carrier checks done once, never re-verified, never evidenced.
- Weighbridge tickets in a shoebox, unlinked to transfer documentation.
- Retention nobody manages — records destroyed too early or unfindable.

## FAQ

**Who does the duty of care apply to?**
Anyone in the waste chain — producer, carrier, keeper, treater, disposer — of controlled waste (EPA 1990 s.34). Householders have a narrower duty under s.34(2).

**Does digital waste tracking replace duty of care records?**
No. DWT adds a mandatory digital record of receipt; the duty of care and its evidence (carrier checks, descriptions, your own records) continue alongside it.
